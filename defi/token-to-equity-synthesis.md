## 1. tl;dr

- **Across Protocol's "Bridge Across" proposal (March 11, 2026) is the first major DAO-to-C-corp conversion attempt**, offering ACX holders 1:1 equity or a $0.04375 USDC buyout (25% premium to 30-day VWAP). Snapshot vote was scheduled for late March/early April 2026; no confirmed public result as of March 31.

- **The proposal arrives after a 98% token decline** (ATH $1.76 in December 2024 to ~$0.035 pre-announcement), unresolved allegations of $23M in DAO treasury manipulation, insider trading accusations related to a Binance listing, and zero protocol revenue -- raising the question of whether this is innovation or distressed restructuring.

- **The GENIUS Act (signed July 2025) covers only payment stablecoins.** It does not address governance tokens, utility tokens, DeFi protocol tokens, or the broader securities classification question. It is largely irrelevant to the token-to-equity debate.

- **The real regulatory event is the March 17, 2026 SEC-CFTC Joint Interpretive Guidance** (Release No. 33-11412) -- a 68-page document creating a five-category token taxonomy and explicitly naming 16 tokens as digital commodities (not securities). Governance tokens are tentatively classified as digital commodities.

- **All current regulatory clarity is executive branch discretion, not statute.** The interpretive guidance can be revised or withdrawn by a future SEC chair without Congressional action. Market structure legislation (FIT21, CLARITY Act, Digital Commodity Intermediaries Act) remains stalled in the Senate.

- **Corrected 2025 crypto M&A and IPO activity totals approximately $23.2B** (~$8.6B in M&A + ~$14.6B in IPOs per The Block), not the $57.1B figure that appeared in earlier research. Digital asset businesses raised $3.4B through IPOs specifically.

- **The 2025 crypto IPO class shows mixed results.** Circle IPO'd at $31, closed day one at $83.23 (+168%), now down ~70% from ATH. Bullish IPO'd at $37, closed day one at $68 (+84%, not the +218% intraday peak sometimes cited). BitGo IPO'd at $18 in January 2026, now at $8.23 (-54%). Kraken paused its IPO despite a $20B valuation and $2.2B in 2025 revenue.

- **Coinbase's S&P 500 inclusion (May 19, 2025) was a watershed moment**, driving an estimated $16B in buying pressure ($9B passive + $7B active per Bernstein) and forcing every U.S. index fund to hold crypto exposure. Coinbase market cap is approximately $40.5B as of March 2026 (not $52B as some sources reported).

- **No protocol has completed the full token-to-equity-to-IPO pipeline.** Every crypto company that went public in 2025 was already structured as a traditional corporation. The Across conversion, if executed, would be the first test case -- but an IPO from converted-token equity remains theoretical.

- **85% of 2025 token launches trade below listing price** (DWF Labs data; the primary source says "over 80%," secondary sources round to 85%). The median drawdown is 70%+ within 90 days. This is the strongest evidence for a structural shift from tokens to equity.

- **DAO governance is empirically broken.** Gini coefficients of 0.97-0.99 across 10 major DAOs (Cambridge research), the Beanstalk $182M flash loan governance attack, the Party Parrot treasury drain, and the Across $23M treasury scandal collectively demonstrate that token governance in practice means plutocratic governance.

- **Paradigm led Across Protocol's $41M funding round** and is the single largest beneficiary if the ACX-to-equity conversion becomes a template. a16z Crypto is raising a $2B fifth fund. The institutional narrative around token-to-equity conversion benefits specific VCs but the underlying data on token underperformance is genuine.

- **Backpack Exchange (BP token, launched March 23, 2026) represents the design-from-scratch model** -- equity conversion built into tokenomics from day one, zero insider allocation at TGE, milestone-based unlocks, and IPO-price priority for one-year stakers. It is days old and completely untested.

- **The honest answer on tokens vs. equity:** for many governance tokens, the distinction from equity is primarily a legal/regulatory arbitrage, not a functional one. Smart contracts can replicate all equity rights. The Across conversion may be admitting what was always true.

## 2. The Across "Bridge Across" Proposal -- What Actually Happened

### The Mechanics (Verified)

On March 11, 2026, Risk Labs posted "The Bridge Across" to the Across governance forum, proposing the protocol transition from a token-based DAO to a U.S. C-corporation ("AcrossCo").

**Conversion terms:**
- 1:1 token-to-share ratio for all holders
- Holders above 5M ACX: direct equity conversion into AcrossCo shares
- Holders between 250K-5M ACX: equity through a no-fee Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV)
- USDC buyout alternative: $0.04375 per token (25% premium to 30-day VWAP)
- Accredited investor caps (~100 U.S. investors, ~500 international) imply reliance on Regulation D (Rule 506) exemptions
- "All token holders -- institutional investors, employees, everyday token holders -- treated the same"

**What is NOT confirmed:** Whether equity is common or preferred stock; specific voting/governance rights; whether Paradigm or other VCs negotiated side agreements; the specific law firm providing counsel; Delaware entity filing details; whether the Snapshot vote has passed (vote was scheduled for March 26 or April 2, sources diverge).

### The Context the Announcement Did Not Emphasize

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| ACX all-time high | $1.76 (December 2024) |
| ACX price before announcement | ~$0.035 |
| Decline from ATH | ~98% |
| Price after announcement (+85% surge) | ~$0.064 (still 96% below ATH) |
| USDC buyout price | $0.04375 (97.5% below ATH) |
| Protocol revenue | $0 (fees go to relayers/LPs) |
| Lifetime bridge volume | $35B+ |
| DAO manipulation allegations | ~$23M in questioned treasury transfers |
| Insider trading allegations | Binance listing front-running claims (denied by team) |
| Total funding raised | $51M (Paradigm lead) |
| Security exploits | Zero (18 audits, 232 issues addressed) |

**Assessment:** The Across case has clear elements of distressed restructuring dressed as innovation. A 98% token decline, governance scandals, and zero protocol revenue create strong incentives for any alternative structure. However, the protocol does have genuine technical merit ($35B in bridge volume, zero exploits, co-authored ERC-7683 cross-chain intents standard adopted by 35+ L2s). The question is whether the conversion is a vote of confidence in the underlying technology or an admission that the token model failed for this specific project.

### Innovation or Desperation?

**Desperation signals:**
- 98% token decline
- $23M treasury manipulation allegations
- Zero protocol revenue
- Buyout price crystallizes massive losses for anyone who bought above $0.05
- Paradigm (lead investor) needs an exit path from an illiquid governance token

**Innovation signals:**

- Real protocol usage and technical merit (ERC-7683, $35B volume)
- Addresses well-documented structural limitations of DAOs for enterprise partnerships
- Could unlock institutional capital and enforceable contracts

## 3. The Regulatory Landscape -- What's Actually Settled

### Layer 1: The GENIUS Act (Stablecoins Only)

Signed July 18, 2025. Passed Senate 68-30, House 308-122. Covers **only** payment stablecoins:
- 1:1 reserve backing required
- Bank subsidiaries overseen by primary regulator; nonbank issuers by OCC; state-licensed under $10B by states
- Payment stablecoins explicitly NOT securities or commodities
- AML/BSA compliance required
- Effective January 18, 2027 (or 120 days after final regulations)

**What it does NOT cover:** governance tokens, utility tokens, DeFi protocols, the securities question, market structure, DEX regulation, NFTs, token issuance frameworks. It is entirely irrelevant to the token-to-equity debate.

### Layer 2: March 17, 2026 SEC-CFTC Joint Interpretive Guidance

Release No. 33-11412 -- a 68-page joint interpretive document that is the most consequential regulatory development for non-stablecoin tokens since 2019.

**The five-category token taxonomy:**

| Category | Regulatory Status | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Digital Commodities | NOT securities; CFTC oversight | 16 named tokens + governance tokens broadly |
| Digital Collectibles | NOT securities | NFTs, digital art, gaming items |
| Digital Tools | NOT securities | Memberships, tickets, credentials |
| Stablecoins | NOT securities (per GENIUS Act) | USDC, USDT |
| Digital Securities | Securities; full SEC jurisdiction | Tokens with explicit profit-sharing or equity-like rights |

**16 explicitly named digital commodities:** BTC (already understood), ETH, SOL, XRP, ADA, LINK, AVAX, DOT, XLM, HBAR, LTC, DOGE, SHIB, XTZ, BCH, APT, ALGO.

**What it clears:** Protocol staking, mining, no-consideration airdrops, wrapping, bridges -- all not securities transactions.

**What it DOESN'T provide:**
- A bright-line test for "sufficient decentralization"
- Coverage for all tokens (thousands remain unclassified)
- A clear boundary between digital commodity and digital security for tokens with governance + revenue features
- Legal durability -- this is interpretive guidance, not statute

### Layer 3: Hester Peirce Safe Harbor / "Regulation Crypto"

Chairman Atkins proposed "Regulation Crypto Assets" on March 17, 2026, building on Commissioner Peirce's prior Token Safe Harbor (Rule 195) proposals:
- Startup exemption: up to 4 years, raises up to $5M
- Fundraising exemption: up to $75M in any 12-month period
- Investment contract safe harbor: criteria for when a token exits securities classification
- Innovation exemption framework for testing novel business models

**Status:** Proposed framework for future rulemaking. Not yet formally proposed for public comment. Not law.

### Layer 4: Market Structure Legislation (Still Not Passed)

| Bill | Status |
|---|---|
| FIT21 (H.R. 4763) | House passed May 2024, 279-136. Stalled in Senate. Superseded. |
| CLARITY Act (H.R. 3633) | House passed July 2025, 294-134. Awaiting Senate action. |
| Digital Commodity Intermediaries Act | Senate Ag Committee passed 12-11 (Jan 29, 2026). First crypto bill through a Senate committee. |
| Senate Banking Committee bill | Markup announced for Jan 15, 2026, postponed. 100+ amendments filed. |

**Timeline assessment:** Optimistic -- floor vote summer 2026, reconciliation by year-end. Realistic -- pushed to 2027 by Senate campaign season. Pessimistic -- dies in the 119th Congress.

### The Key Vulnerability

All current regulatory clarity beyond stablecoins is executive branch discretion. The five-category taxonomy, the 16 digital commodity designations, the enforcement stand-down, and the proposed safe harbors exist because the current SEC chairman wants them to exist. A change in administration could reverse course without any Congressional action required. The interpretive guidance has not been tested in court. Until market structure legislation is enacted, the entire framework is fragile.

## 4. Tokens vs Equity -- The Actual Functional Analysis

### What Equity Can Do That Tokens Structurally Cannot

| Capability | Equity | Governance Tokens |
|---|---|---|
| Enforceable contracts & legal standing | Yes -- shareholders can sue | Tokens confer no legal standing in most jurisdictions |
| Fiduciary duties | Directors owe duties to shareholders | No enforceable fiduciary standard in DAOs |
| Dividends / profit distribution | Legally enforceable | Possible via smart contracts but no legal obligation; creates securities exposure |
| M&A participation | Shareholders receive acquisition premiums | No "tender offer" mechanism for DAO tokens |
| Index inclusion | S&P 500, Russell 2000, etc. | Impossible |
| Institutional access | Pension funds, endowments, mutual funds, insurance cos | Most mandates prohibit direct token exposure |
| Banking relationships | Full access to commercial banking, credit facilities | Severely limited |
| Employee compensation | RSUs, ISOs, NSOs with established tax treatment | Token grants face uncertain tax treatment |
| Insurance (D&O, E&O, cyber) | Standard | Limited or unavailable for DAOs |
| Regulatory clarity | Well-established securities law | Evolving; guidance-dependent |

### What Tokens Can Do That Equity Cannot

| Capability | Tokens | Equity |
|---|---|---|
| 24/7 global liquidity | Yes -- permissionless, no broker, no geographic restriction | Market hours, brokerage required, T+1 settlement |
| Composability | Collateral, staking, LP, integration into DeFi | Not natively composable (but tokenized equity platforms are emerging) |
| Global distribution | Airdrops reach worldwide | Strict geographical restrictions |
| Programmatic governance | On-chain voting, automated execution | Board votes, proxy processes |

**These advantages are eroding.** Tokenized equity platforms (Securitize, PreStocks), extended-hours trading, and the SEC's March 2026 joint guidance on tokenized securities are creating a regulatory path for 24/7 equity trading on-chain.

### The Honest Comparison Table

| Feature | Governance Token (Typical) | Common Equity | Real Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voting rights | Yes (protocol parameters) | Yes (board, major decisions) | Token votes narrower in scope |
| Economic exposure | Token price tracks protocol value | Share price tracks company value | Minimal in practice |
| Revenue share | Sometimes ("real yield") | Yes (dividends) | When revenue share exists, the difference is essentially zero |
| Liquidation preference | No formal rights | Yes (statutory) | Significant legal gap |
| Legal ownership | No ownership of underlying entity | Fractional ownership | Fundamental structural difference |
| Regulatory protection | Minimal | Full securities law protections | Massive gap in investor protection |
| Transferability | Permissionless, 24/7, global | Restricted unless public; T+1 | Token is more liquid (for now) |

### DAO Governance Failures

The empirical record is damning:

- **Gini coefficients of 0.97-0.99** across 10 major DAOs (Cambridge research). MakerDAO: 0.99. Rocket Pool: 0.97. For comparison, the most unequal nation on earth (South Africa) has a Gini of 0.63.
- **Beanstalk ($182M, April 2022):** Attacker used $1B in flash loans to gain 67% governance control and drain reserves. No equivalent attack exists in equity structures.
- **Party Parrot:** Team unlocked tokens giving them 80% of voting power, then voted to distribute ~$60M from a ~$70M treasury to themselves.
- **Across Protocol ($23M, June 2025):** Allegations that Risk Labs used insider-controlled wallets to pass governance proposals directing $23M from the DAO treasury to Risk Labs. Unresolved.
- **UMA ($7M, March 2025):** A single whale accumulated 25% of voting power and forced an incorrect oracle resolution on Polymarket. Largest single voter controlled the outcome.

## 6. The Philosophical Question: Is This Just a Branding Problem?

### If everything is on-chain, are tokens just poorly labeled equity?

For many governance tokens, the answer is closer to yes than the industry admits. The functional comparison in Section 4 shows that the real differences between governance tokens and common equity are:

1. **Legal ownership** -- equity confers fractional ownership of an entity; tokens typically do not
2. **Regulatory protection** -- equity holders benefit from securities laws, fiduciary duties, and courts; token holders have smart contracts and governance forums
3. **Liquidation preference** -- equity has statutory priority claims; tokens have nothing

Beyond these, the differences are largely:
- **Transferability** (token advantage, eroding)
- **Composability** (token advantage, eroding)
- **Global access** (token advantage, eroding)

### Were SAFTs always unregistered securities with extra steps?

Functionally, yes. The SAFT framework was a legal fiction that attempted to separate the "investment contract" wrapper from the underlying token. Courts consistently rejected this separation:
- **Telegram (2020):** Court held the SAFT-like structure was inseparable from the tokens
- **Kik (2020):** Court looked at economic substance over labels
- **Ripple (2023-2025):** Partially vindicated the distinction for programmatic exchange sales, but this was a single district court ruling later settled

The SEC's consistent position across both the Gensler and pre-Gensler eras was that the SAFT separation was formalistic, not substantive. The manner of distribution matters as much as the characteristics of the token.

### Does the March 2026 guidance settle this?

Partially. The guidance classifies governance tokens as digital commodities based on the premise that their value derives from "the programmatic operation of a functional crypto system" and "supply and demand dynamics" rather than managerial efforts. But for many DeFi protocols:
- A small core team does most of the development
- Token holders rarely exercise meaningful governance (see Gini data)
- Token value correlates directly with protocol revenue generated by the team
- "Decentralization" is often more formal than functional

The guidance works because the current administration wants it to work. Whether it survives adversarial scrutiny from a future enforcement-minded SEC is an open question.

### Can smart contracts replicate all equity rights?

Technically, yes. Smart contracts can encode dividends, voting, pro-rata claims, liquidation waterfalls, transfer restrictions, and vesting schedules. This is what tokenized equity platforms (Securitize, tZERO) already do. The January 28, 2026 SEC joint statement confirmed that tokenizing a security does not change its legal characterization -- stock is equity regardless of format.

### Is the Across conversion admitting what was always true?

If ACX can be converted 1:1 to equity, what was the functional difference between the token and equity? ACX already provided governance rights and economic exposure. The conversion is an acknowledgment that the token was functioning as quasi-equity all along -- minus the legal protections, institutional access, and enforceable rights that equity provides. The "innovation" of the token model, for protocols like Across, was primarily regulatory arbitrage: global distribution without SEC registration. As that arbitrage narrows (through regulatory clarity and tokenized equity platforms), the justification for governance tokens over equity weakens.

## 7. The "Podcast Industrial Complex" Question

### Who Benefits from the Token-to-Equity Narrative?

**Paradigm:**
- Led Across Protocol's $41M round
- Holds ACX tokens (an illiquid, 98%-declined governance token)
- In an equity structure, Paradigm would typically receive preferred stock with liquidation preference, board seats, anti-dilution protection, and registration rights
- They are the single largest beneficiary if ACX-to-equity conversion becomes a template
- The 85% ACX price surge on announcement benefited existing holders

**a16z Crypto:**
- Raising $2B for fifth crypto fund (H1 2026 close target)
- Their "Read Write Own" Web3 thesis has underperformed
- An equity-friendly narrative helps justify new capital allocation and portfolio company restructuring
- Note: the claim in some sources of a16z raising a $20B fund is unverified; this likely conflates cumulative AUM across all a16z funds

**Backpack Exchange / Armani Ferrante:**
- Designed the BP token with equity conversion from day one
- Both practitioner and evangelist of the model

**Block Research / The Block:**
- Published the key framing article ("Is a token-to-equity shift emerging in crypto?")
- Editorial line consistently favorable to the equity narrative

### Is Institutional Demand Real or Manufactured?

**Real signals:**
- 85% of 2025 tokens underwater (DWF Labs)
- ~$23.2B in 2025 M&A/IPO activity (corrected)
- 47% of institutional investors expect 3%+ crypto allocation within three years (EY)
- 65% of institutional investors said crypto will provide the most attractive risk-adjusted returns over five years (EY)
- Coinbase S&P 500 inclusion forced passive allocation

**Manufactured/amplified signals:**
- Token-to-equity benefits VCs who hold both tokens and equity -- they can exit tokens at a premium while retaining equity
- Paradigm's direct involvement in Across means they are financially incentivized to make this narrative succeed
- Crypto media amplifies these stories for engagement, not because they reflect consensus

**What non-crypto-native institutions actually say:**
- Grayscale's 2026 outlook focuses on Bitcoin/Ethereum ETFs and tokenized assets, not governance token-to-equity conversion
- EY's institutional survey found demand concentrated in Bitcoin and Ethereum, not in governance tokens of converting DeFi protocols
- Pantera Capital's 2026 letter focuses on crypto infrastructure embedded in Wall Street products
- The institutional demand is real but channeled through equity and ETF wrappers -- not through converted tokens

**Assessment:** The underlying trend (tokens failing, equity winning) is genuine and well-documented. The specific mechanism (token-to-equity conversion as a template) is an amplified narrative that benefits specific VCs more than the broader market. The simpler story -- build a company, go public, forget the token -- may be more relevant than the conversion pathway.

### The 85% Underwater Stat

Verified via DWF Labs data (primary source says "over 80%," secondary sources round to 85%). Median drawdown: 70%+ within 90 days of TGE. Low-float/high-FDV tokenomics create "phantom valuations" where small price moves imply large FDVs that do not reflect real liquidity. 21.3% of the top 300 tokens by market cap are low-float (circulating supply below 50% of total).

Converting to equity eliminates the unlock schedule problem. A C-corp can issue shares on a controlled schedule through board-approved dilution rather than facing programmatic token emissions that destroy holder value.

### The Headwinds

1. **BitGo's post-IPO collapse (-54%)** poisons the well for the entire pipeline
2. **Kraken's pause** -- if a $20B-valuation exchange with $2.2B revenue cannot IPO, smaller converted-token entities have no realistic near-term path
3. **Legal complexity** -- no precedent exists for token-to-equity conversion; securities law implications are untested
4. **Tax consequences** -- conversion is almost certainly a taxable event (Section 1031 like-kind exchange treatment unavailable for crypto since January 1, 2018)
5. **Not all protocols should convert** -- genuinely decentralized protocols (Bitcoin, Ethereum base layer) would be harmed by corporate structures
6. **VC exit dynamics** -- if Paradigm holds both tokens and equity, the conversion may be structured to benefit them at the expense of token-only holders

### The Honest Assessment

The Across case is more desperation than innovation -- the numbers (98% decline, zero revenue, treasury scandal) tell that story clearly. But the structural arguments (DAOs cannot sign contracts, institutions need corporate counterparties, governance is plutocratic) are genuine and apply beyond Across. The Backpack model, designed from day one, is more intellectually honest. The question is whether the broader market learns from both.

## 9. Strategic Questions That Must Be Addressed

### For the Across Conversion Specifically

1. Did the Snapshot vote pass? What was the turnout and voter distribution?
2. What type of equity (common vs. preferred) do converted holders receive?
3. Did Paradigm or other VCs negotiate side agreements granting them preferred rights despite the "equal treatment" language?
4. What happens to unexchanged tokens and the protocol's smart contracts after conversion?
5. Is AcrossCo pursuing IRC Section 368 tax-free reorganization treatment, or will conversion be a taxable event for all holders?
6. What is Across's path to revenue? If the protocol captures zero fees today, what changes with corporate structure?

### For the Token-to-Equity Thesis Broadly

7. If governance tokens are now classified as digital commodities (not securities), does converting to equity actually increase or decrease regulatory risk?
8. Does the SPV structure for smaller holders (designed to stay under the 500-shareholder rule) create a two-tier system where large holders get direct equity rights and small holders get diluted SPV exposure?
9. How do token-to-equity conversions handle holders in non-U.S. jurisdictions where the resulting equity may be unregisterable?
10. If the token model was always quasi-equity, should new protocols just issue tokenized equity from day one using Securitize or similar platforms?
11. Does the Backpack model (designed equity conversion from inception) create a new standard, or is it a niche experiment?

### For the Regulatory Environment

12. What happens to the five-category token taxonomy if the 2028 election produces a hostile SEC chair?
13. Will market structure legislation actually pass the Senate before the 119th Congress expires?
14. How does the "sufficiently decentralized" standard get defined? Without a bright-line test, protocol teams cannot know when they have crossed the threshold.
15. If the SEC's Regulation Crypto rulemaking formalizes the safe harbor, does it survive an administration change?

### For Institutional Investors

16. Is there genuine allocator demand for converted-token equity, or is this a narrative created by crypto-native VCs for crypto-native LPs?
17. What due diligence framework should institutions use to evaluate a protocol that was a DAO last quarter and is now a C-corp?
18. How should institutions price the risk that the regulatory framework supporting the conversion could be reversed?
19. What is the realistic timeline from conversion to IPO, if one exists?
20. Does the Across treasury scandal disqualify this specific conversion as a precedent, or does the institutional market not care about crypto-native governance history?
