## CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### 1. GENIUS Act Covers ONLY Payment Stablecoins

**Verdict: VERIFIED -- ACCURATE**

The GENIUS Act (S.1582) was signed into law on July 18, 2025, after passing the Senate 68-30 on June 17, 2025, and the House 308-122 on July 17, 2025. The bill's scope is limited to "payment stablecoins" -- digital assets redeemable at a fixed monetary value. The report's claim that it does not address governance tokens, utility tokens, DeFi protocol tokens, or the broader securities classification question is confirmed by the White House fact sheet, Latham & Watkins analysis, and the legislative text on Congress.gov.

The report's description of reserve requirements (1:1 backing), issuer oversight structure (bank subsidiaries, OCC for nonbanks, state regulators under $10B), securities/commodities exemption, AML/BSA compliance, consumer protection (priority claims in bankruptcy), and enforcement tools (freeze/seize/burn capability) all match the enacted provisions.

The effective date ("January 18, 2027, or 120 days after final regulations are issued, whichever comes first") is confirmed. The statute says the earlier of 18 months after enactment or 120 days after final implementing regulations, which aligns with the report.

**No corrections needed.**

### 3. The Five-Category Token Taxonomy

**Verdict: VERIFIED -- ACCURATE**

The five categories are confirmed across multiple law firm analyses:

1. **Digital Commodities** -- crypto assets linked to programmatic operation of a functional crypto system
2. **Digital Collectibles** -- artwork, music, gaming items, memes
3. **Digital Tools** -- memberships, tickets, credentials, identity badges
4. **Stablecoins** -- covered by GENIUS Act
5. **Digital Securities** -- investment contracts under Howey

The report's descriptions of each category match the guidance as analyzed by Ropes & Gray, Jenner & Block, Baker Botts, and others. The acknowledgment that "some crypto assets may not fit neatly into any category and that others may have hybrid characteristics" is confirmed in the law firm analyses.

**No corrections needed.**

### 4. The 16 Named Digital Commodities

**Verdict: VERIFIED WITH MINOR NOTE**

The report lists: XRP, ETH, SOL, ADA, LINK, AVAX, DOT, XLM, HBAR, LTC, DOGE, SHIB, XTZ, BCH, APT, ALGO.

This list of 16 is confirmed by CoinPedia, Finance Police, Disruption Banking, and other sources covering the guidance. The report's table in Section 2 includes BTC in the "Examples" column alongside the 16 named tokens, which could create confusion -- it should be clearer that BTC was already classified and the 16 are the *newly* named tokens.

**Minor correction recommended:** Clarify that BTC was already classified and the 16 are the new additions. The report's table mixes BTC into the "Examples" column alongside the newly named 16, which is slightly misleading about the scope of the new guidance.

### 9. Telegram Case

**Verdict: ERROR DETECTED -- "$1.7B settlement" is WRONG**

The report in the tl;dr does not mention Telegram with a "$1.7B" figure, but it does reference Telegram in Section 6. Let me be precise about what the report says and what actually happened:

**What happened:**
- Telegram raised $1.7 billion through its TON offering (the *offering amount*, not a settlement)
- The SEC obtained a court order halting the distribution of Gram tokens
- Settlement (June 2020): Telegram agreed to return $1.2 billion to investors AND pay an $18.5 million penalty
- The total settlement was approximately $1.22 billion disgorgement + $18.5 million penalty

**What the report says (Section 6):** The report discusses Telegram accurately in the context of the SAFT framework, correctly noting the court held the arrangement was an unregistered securities offering. The report does NOT claim a "$1.7B settlement" -- the audit question mentioned this figure, but the report itself does not use it. The report's treatment of Telegram is factually accurate.

**No corrections needed in the report itself.** The "$1.7B" figure referenced in the audit question does not appear in the document. The $1.7B was the amount raised, not the settlement amount.

### 12. "Regulation Crypto" Rulemaking

**Verdict: VERIFIED -- REAL INITIATIVE, ACCURATELY DESCRIBED**

"Regulation Crypto" (or "Regulation Crypto Assets") is a real proposed rulemaking initiative announced by SEC Chairman Atkins in his March 17, 2026 remarks at The Digital Chamber's 2026 Blockchain Summit. It is confirmed by SEC.gov, Greenberg Traurig, Ledger Insights, and multiple other sources. Key components:

- Startup exemption (time-limited, up to $5M raises with principles-based disclosures)
- Investment contract safe harbor (rule-based standard for when a token is no longer a security)
- Mature network exemption (defined window for early-stage projects)

The report accurately describes this as "proposed rulemaking (forthcoming)" and "not yet finalized." It has NOT been formally proposed for public comment yet -- the SEC is expected to release proposed rule text in "coming weeks" as of March 2026.

**No corrections needed. Accurately described as forthcoming, not finalized.**

### 14. SEC Enforcement Posture Under Atkins

**Verdict: VERIFIED WITH ONE CORRECTION NEEDED**

- **Coinbase dismissal (Feb 27, 2025):** Confirmed via SEC.gov press release.
- **Ripple settlement:** The report says "$125M penalty." Verified -- the court ordered a civil penalty of $125 million. Both sides dropped appeals in August 2025. However, one nuance: the original court-ordered penalty was ~$125M (from Judge Torres' August 2024 ruling), and the 2025 settlement involved both sides dropping appeals and the SEC granting a "bad actor" waiver. The report's characterization of "narrow injunction limited to institutional sales" is accurate.
- **Other case closures (Binance, Gemini, Robinhood, Kraken):** Confirmed by Yahoo Finance, Harvard Law Forum, and SEC.gov records.
- **"Project Crypto" launched July 31, 2025:** Confirmed via SEC.gov.
- **November 2025 speech detailing Project Crypto:** Confirmed (November 12, 2025, per Sidley Austin and SEC.gov).

**Minor correction:** The report says "both sides dropped appeals in August 2025." The SEC announced the settlement on May 8, 2025 (per Commissioner Crenshaw's dissent statement), and the formal dismissal of appeals was filed on June 27, 2025, with court approval following. The report should say the case was settled in May 2025 with appeals formally dismissed by mid-2025, not specifically "August 2025."
