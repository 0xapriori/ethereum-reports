# The Crypto-to-Public-Markets Pipeline: Token as Springboard to IPO

**Research Brief | March 31, 2026**

**tl;dr**

- **The crypto IPO wave is real but fragile.** Circle ($23B market cap), Galaxy Digital (Nasdaq), and BitGo (NYSE, now at $8.23 vs $18 IPO price) have gone public; Kraken ($20B valuation) paused its IPO; Consensys, Ledger, and Anchorage are in the pipeline for 2026-2027.
- **85% of 2025 token launches trade below listing price.** Institutional capital is measurably shifting from token exposure to crypto equity exposure through IPOs, ETFs, and index inclusion (Coinbase joined the S&P 500 in May 2025).
- **The token-to-equity conversion is an emerging but contested pattern.** Across Protocol (ACX) and Backpack (BP) are pioneering token-for-equity swap mechanisms, but both carry significant baggage -- ACX had a DAO manipulation scandal, and the model remains legally untested at scale.
- **Equity structurally outperforms tokens for institutional access.** Dividends, M&A participation, index inclusion, enforceable contracts, fiduciary duties, and banking relationships are all structurally impossible or severely limited with governance tokens.
- **The narrative is partly manufactured, partly real.** Paradigm and a16z are positioned to benefit from the token-to-equity shift, but the underlying data on token underperformance and institutional preference for equity is genuine, not just VC spin.

## 2. Token as Springboard / Filtering Mechanism Theory

### The Core Question: Can a Token Launch Serve as a Proving Ground Before Going Public?

The theory goes: launch a token to prove product-market fit, build a user base, generate revenue metrics, then convert to equity for institutional access. Two protocols are now actively testing this thesis.

### Across Protocol (ACX) -- The First Major Test Case

**What happened:**

- Across Protocol, a Paradigm-backed cross-chain bridge, posted a "temperature check" proposal in March 2026 to convert from a DAO to a U.S. C-corporation ("AcrossCo").
- ACX holders would get two options: (1) exchange ACX 1:1 for AcrossCo equity, or (2) redeem ACX for USDC at $0.04375 (25% premium to 30-day average market price).
- Community call scheduled March 18; Snapshot vote March 26.
- ACX surged ~80-85% on the announcement, with trading volume hitting 3.5x market value.

**The stated rationale (from Risk Labs / Across team):**
> "As Across deepens our work with institutional and enterprise partners, the token and DAO structure has materially impacted our ability to close partnerships and integrations. Transitioning to a traditional legal entity would meaningfully improve our ability to enter enforceable contracts, structure revenue agreements, and deliver more value to Across stakeholders."

**Critical context the announcement did not emphasize:**

- ACX hit an ATH of $1.76 in December 2024. By March 2026, it was trading around $0.035 -- a decline of approximately **98%**.
- In June 2025, Across Protocol faced serious allegations of DAO governance manipulation. Pseudonymous founder Ogle of Glue accused the Risk Labs team of using insider-controlled wallets to pass governance proposals transferring ~$23M in ACX tokens from the community treasury to Risk Labs. Two proposals were at the center: 100M ACX ($15M) and another worth $7.5M.
- LayerZero founder Bryan Pellegrino alleged that Risk Labs CEO Hart Lambur purchased ACX tokens before a surprise Binance listing in December 2024, suggesting insider trading.
- Risk Labs co-founder Hart Lambur refuted all allegations, stating Risk Labs is a Cayman-based nonprofit and the team was unaware of the Binance listing.
- ACX dropped 10%+ on the scandal before the March 2026 equity conversion announcement reversed the price action.

**Assessment:** The Across case is not a clean test of the token-to-equity thesis. The token had lost 98% of its value, the DAO faced manipulation allegations, and the conversion offer of $0.04375 in USDC is itself a fraction of the token's former highs. It is at least partially a restructuring born of necessity, dressed up as innovation.

### Backpack (BP) -- The Designed-From-Scratch Model

**What happened:**

- Backpack Exchange launched the BP token on Solana on March 23, 2026.
- The token was designed with an equity conversion mechanism from day one: users who stake BP for one year can convert tokens to Backpack company equity, representing up to 20% of the company in aggregate.
- Stakers also get priority allocation for shares at IPO price.
- 25% of supply released at TGE, all to users. Zero allocation to founders, employees, or VCs at TGE.
- 37.5% unlocks based on operational milestones; 37.5% locked in corporate treasury until after potential IPO.
- Team members hold equity in the company; the company holds the majority of token supply.

**Assessment:** This is the more intellectually honest version of the token-to-equity model. The conversion was designed into the tokenomics from inception, not retrofitted after a 98% decline. The zero-insider-allocation at TGE is a meaningful structural choice. However, the model is days old (launched March 23, 2026) and completely untested -- we cannot yet know if it works.

### What Metrics from Token/Protocol Success Translate to Public Market Readiness?

Based on the patterns observed:

| Token-Era Metric | Public Market Translation |
|---|---|
| Total Value Locked (TVL) | Comparable to Assets Under Management (AUM) |
| Protocol revenue (fees) | Direct revenue line item |
| Daily/monthly active users | Customer base / user engagement |
| Transaction volume | Gross merchandise volume or transaction throughput |
| Developer activity | R&D investment / technical moat |
| Security audit history | Risk management maturity |
| Governance participation rates | Stakeholder engagement |
| Cross-chain integrations | Distribution partnerships |

### Is the Token-to-Traction-to-Revenue-to-Equity-to-IPO Pipeline a Real Pattern?

**Not yet, definitively.** No protocol has completed the full pipeline from token launch through equity conversion to IPO. The closest examples:

- Circle had USDC (not a governance token, but a stablecoin product) before IPO.
- Coinbase was never a token-first project.
- Across and Backpack are attempting the conversion but have not reached IPO.

The pattern exists as a thesis, not as a proven pathway. It is better described as an **emerging structural experiment** than an established pipeline.

## 3. Why Equity Over Tokens -- The Actual Benefits

### Structural Capabilities: What Equity Can Do That Tokens Cannot

| Capability | Equity | Governance Tokens | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dividends / profit distribution** | Yes, legally enforceable | Possible via smart contracts but no legal obligation | Token dividends create securities law exposure |
| **M&A participation (acquisition premium)** | Yes -- shareholders receive premium in acquisitions | No mechanism; token may become worthless post-acquisition | This is a massive gap; there is no "tender offer" for DAO tokens |
| **Board governance & fiduciary duties** | Yes -- directors owe fiduciary duties to shareholders | DAO governance has no enforceable fiduciary standard | Gini coefficients of 0.97-0.99 in major DAOs show extreme concentration |
| **Enforceable contracts & legal standing** | Yes -- shareholders can sue, enforce contracts | Tokens do not confer legal standing in most jurisdictions | This was Across Protocol's stated reason for converting |
| **Institutional investor access** | Pension funds, endowments, mutual funds, insurance companies | Extremely limited; most institutional mandates prohibit direct token exposure | Even crypto-friendly institutions prefer equity wrappers |
| **Index inclusion** | S&P 500, Russell 2000, etc. | Impossible | Coinbase's S&P 500 inclusion drove ~$16B in passive buying |
| **Employee compensation** | RSUs, ISOs, NSOs with established tax treatment | Token grants face uncertain tax treatment and lockup complexity | Recruiting advantage for equity-based companies |
| **Banking relationships** | Full access to commercial banking, credit facilities | Severely limited; many banks will not bank token projects | Circle's revenue model depends on bank partnerships |
| **Insurance** | D&O, E&O, cyber, key person -- all standard | Limited or unavailable for DAO structures | This affects ability to attract board members |
| **Regulatory clarity** | Well-established securities law framework | Evolving; SEC/CFTC joint guidance (Mar 2026) helps but still uncertain | 18 major tokens designated "digital commodities" in March 2026 |

### The Liquidity Tradeoff

Tokens have one genuine structural advantage: 24/7 global liquidity with no broker, settlement delay, or geographic restriction. Equity trades during market hours, requires brokerage accounts, and settles T+1 (or T+0 for tokenized equity).

However, this advantage is eroding:
- Tokenized equity platforms (e.g., PreStocks) are bringing equity onchain.
- Extended-hours trading on traditional exchanges is expanding.
- The SEC's March 2026 joint guidance on tokenized securities is creating a regulatory path for 24/7 equity trading.

### Real Examples of Protocols Struggling Because They Only Had Tokens

**Across Protocol:** Explicitly stated that the DAO and token structure "materially impacted our ability to close partnerships and integrations." B2B infrastructure requiring enforceable contracts, SLAs, and counterparty liability could not function effectively under a DAO.

**Beanstalk Protocol:** Suffered a $182M governance attack via flash loan -- an attacker borrowed enough governance tokens to instantly pass a malicious proposal draining reserves. No equivalent attack vector exists in equity structures with fiduciary duties and legal oversight.

**Party Parrot DAO:** The project team held 80% of voting power and successfully passed a vote to drain the treasury. Again, this would constitute fraud in a corporate structure with board duties.

**Aave Governance:** A Lido advisor publicly argued that combining governance tokens with separate equity entities creates "fundamentally broken" incentive misalignment.

**DAO Inequality Data:** Research found Gini coefficients of 0.97-0.99 across 10 major DAOs (MakerDAO at 0.99, Rocket Pool at 0.97), far exceeding the most unequal nation on earth (South Africa at 0.63). Token governance in practice means plutocratic governance.

## 4. The "Podcast Industrial Complex" Question

### Who Is Pushing the Token-to-Equity Narrative?

**Primary amplifiers:**

1. **Paradigm** -- Directly backed Across Protocol ($41M round). They are the single largest beneficiary if the ACX-to-equity conversion becomes a template. Their portfolio would benefit from a systematic shift toward equity structures, as it would unlock institutional capital for their investments.

2. **a16z Crypto** -- Raising $2B for their fifth fund (targeting H1 2026 close). Their "Web3" thesis (Chris Dixon's Read Write Own) has underperformed, with backed projects like Farcaster struggling. A pivot toward equity-friendly structures would help portfolio companies attract institutional capital. However, a16z is also notably raising a $20B fund that includes broader crypto exposure, suggesting they are hedging.

3. **Block Research / The Block** -- Published the key framing article "Is a token-to-equity shift emerging in crypto?" and predicted "IPOs may outperform token launches" in their 2026 predictions. The Block's editorial line has been consistently favorable to the equity narrative.

4. **Backpack Exchange / Armani Ferrante** -- Designed the BP token with equity conversion from day one, making them both a practitioner and an evangelist of the model.

### Is There Genuine Institutional Demand or Manufactured Consensus?

**The data supporting genuine demand:**

- 85% of 2025 token launches trading below listing price (DWF Labs data).
- Crypto M&A and IPO activity reached $57.1B in 2025.
- 47% of institutional investors expect at least 3% crypto portfolio allocation within three years (EY survey).
- 65% of institutional investors said crypto will provide the most attractive risk-adjusted returns over five years (ahead of private equity at 61%).
- Coinbase S&P 500 inclusion forced passive allocation.
- Capital flight from tokens to equity is documented by multiple independent data sources.

**The data suggesting manufactured or amplified consensus:**

- The token-to-equity shift benefits VCs who hold both tokens and equity in portfolio companies. They can exit token positions at a premium (see ACX's 80% pump) while retaining equity.
- Paradigm's direct involvement in Across Protocol means they are financially incentivized to make this narrative succeed.
- a16z is simultaneously raising its largest fund ever while its prior thesis (decentralized Web3) has underperformed -- equity-friendly narratives help justify new capital allocation.
- Crypto media outlets (The Block, Bankless, CoinDesk) are amplifying these stories because they generate engagement, not necessarily because they reflect consensus.

**What do actual institutional investors (not crypto-native VCs) say?**

- Grayscale Research's 2026 outlook calls it the "Dawn of the Institutional Era" but focuses on Bitcoin/Ethereum ETFs and tokenized assets, not governance token-to-equity conversion.
- SVB's 2026 crypto outlook emphasizes stablecoin infrastructure and regulated products, not token restructuring.
- Pantera Capital's 2026 letter focuses on crypto infrastructure embedded in Wall Street products, suggesting institutional demand is real but channeled through equity and ETF wrappers, not converted tokens.
- EY's institutional survey found demand concentrated in Bitcoin and Ethereum -- not in governance tokens of DeFi protocols converting to equity.

**Assessment:** The institutional demand for crypto equity exposure is genuine. The demand for converted-token-to-equity exposure is unproven and may be a niche narrative that benefits specific VCs more than the broader market. The "podcast industrial complex" is amplifying a real trend (tokens failing, equity winning) but may be overstating the specific mechanism (token-to-equity conversion) relative to the simpler story (build a company, go public, forget the token).

## 5. Is Token-to-Equity Desperation or Innovation?

### ACX Context: The Desperation Case

| Metric | Value | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| ATH price | $1.76 (Dec 2024) | Reference point for loss |
| Price before equity announcement | ~$0.035 | 98% decline from ATH |
| Price after equity announcement | ~$0.064 (+85%) | Still 96% below ATH |
| USDC buyout offer | $0.04375 | 25% premium to 30-day avg; 97.5% below ATH |
| DAO manipulation allegations | $23M in questioned treasury transfers | Governance credibility damaged |
| Insider trading allegations | Binance listing front-running claims | Denied by team; unresolved |
| Total funding raised | $51M (led by Paradigm) | VC needs an exit path |
| Protocol volume processed | $35B+ lifetime | Real usage exists |

The ACX case has clear elements of desperation: a 98% token decline, governance scandals, and a buyout price that crystallizes massive losses for anyone who bought above $0.05. However, the protocol does have genuine usage ($35B in bridge volume) and co-created the ERC-7683 cross-chain intents standard, suggesting real technical value underneath the token dysfunction.

### The Low-Float / High-FDV Escape Hatch Theory

One underexplored angle: converting to equity could be a way to escape the structural problems of low-float / high-FDV tokenomics.

Key data:
- 21.3% of the top 300 crypto tokens by market cap are low-float (circulating supply < 50% of total).
- Low-float tokens create "phantom valuations" where small price moves imply large FDVs that don't reflect real liquidity.
- 85% of 2025 TGEs underwater, with median drawdown of 70%+ within 90 days.
- Emission schedules and supply unlocks are the dominant force in 2026 altcoin pricing.

Converting to equity eliminates the unlock schedule problem entirely. A C-corp can issue shares on a known, controlled schedule through board-approved dilution, rather than facing programmatic token emissions that destroy holder value.

**Are there other tokens with declining value that might convert?**

The most likely candidates, per Block Research analysis: "projects that already operate like businesses, with a core team driving execution and revenue, often infrastructure or middleware protocols serving institutional or enterprise clients." Cross-chain bridges and interoperability protocols are the most obvious category.

This description fits a number of protocols beyond Across, though none have publicly announced similar plans as of March 31, 2026.

### The Innovation Case

Arguments that this is genuinely structural, not desperate:

1. **Regulatory clarity is arriving.** The SEC/CFTC joint classification of 18 tokens as "digital commodities" (March 17, 2026) creates the first real legal framework. This makes the equity-vs-token distinction sharper and more navigable.

2. **Backpack designed it from scratch.** The BP token launched with equity conversion as a feature, not a retrofit. Zero insider allocation, milestone-based unlocks, IPO-price priority for stakers. This is not desperation -- it is a deliberate design choice by a team that could have done a standard token launch.

3. **The DAO model has demonstrated failures.** Governance attack vectors (Beanstalk, Party Parrot), extreme wealth concentration (Gini 0.97-0.99), inability to sign enforceable contracts -- these are structural, not fixable with better tokenomics.

4. **Public market infrastructure is ready.** With Circle, BitGo, and Galaxy Digital publicly listed, and Coinbase in the S&P 500, the capital markets infrastructure for crypto companies exists in a way it did not in 2021.

### The Headwinds and Problems with This Thesis

1. **BitGo's post-IPO collapse.** Down 54% from IPO price. If the first crypto IPO of 2026 is a disaster, it poisons the well for everyone behind them.

2. **Kraken's pause.** Even at $20B valuation and $1.5B revenue, market conditions were too hostile. If Kraken cannot IPO, smaller protocols converting from tokens to equity have no realistic IPO path.

3. **Legal complexity of token-to-equity conversion.** No precedent exists. Securities law implications of offering equity in exchange for tokens are untested. The SEC has not opined on this specific mechanism.

4. **Tax consequences for token holders.** Converting tokens to equity may be a taxable event. Holders who bought ACX at $1.76 and convert to equity at $0.04375 face complex tax situations.

5. **Equity is not inherently better for all protocols.** Genuinely decentralized protocols (Bitcoin, Ethereum base layer) would be harmed by corporate structures. The equity advantage applies primarily to protocols that are already operated by identifiable teams -- which raises the question of why they had tokens in the first place.

6. **The VC exit problem.** If Paradigm holds both ACX tokens and Risk Labs equity, the token-to-equity conversion could be structured to benefit them at the expense of smaller holders who only hold tokens. The 25% USDC premium buyout may not reflect the value embedded in the equity conversion option.

7. **Market timing risk.** Going public into a crypto downturn (Bitcoin peaked in late 2024, now significantly below) means compressed valuations. IPO windows are narrow and unpredictable.

## Key Takeaways

### What is real:
- The crypto IPO pipeline is genuine. Multiple companies with real revenue and institutional advisors are preparing to list.
- Institutional preference for equity over tokens is supported by hard data (85% of tokens underwater, $57.1B in M&A/IPO activity).
- The structural limitations of governance tokens (no legal standing, no fiduciary duties, governance attacks, plutocratic concentration) are well-documented.
- Coinbase's S&P 500 inclusion permanently changed how allocators think about crypto exposure.

### What is unproven:
- Whether token-to-equity conversion specifically (as opposed to just building companies and going public) is a viable pathway.
- Whether Across Protocol's conversion will succeed and become a template.
- Whether Backpack's designed-from-scratch model performs better than retrofit conversions.
- Whether the next wave of crypto IPOs performs better than BitGo.

### What is likely manufactured or amplified:
- The framing of token-to-equity conversion as "visionary" when the primary beneficiaries are VCs who hold both tokens and equity.
- The sense of urgency around this narrative, which may be driven by VC fund-raising cycles more than market fundamentals.
- The implication that most protocols should convert, when the evidence suggests only B2B infrastructure protocols with identifiable teams and enterprise clients are good candidates.
