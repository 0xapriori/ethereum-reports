# Token-to-Equity Conversion Mechanics, Precedents, and the Path to IPO

**Research Brief | March 31, 2026**

**tl;dr**

- **Across Protocol's "Bridge Across" proposal is the first major DAO-to-C-corp conversion attempt**, offering ACX holders 1:1 equity or a $0.04375 USDC buyout (25% premium); the Snapshot vote was scheduled for late March/early April 2026, with the result not yet publicly confirmed at time of research.
- **The token-to-equity trend is accelerating**, driven by practical limitations of DAO structures in closing institutional deals, but is most applicable to infrastructure/middleware protocols rather than credibly neutral DeFi primitives.
- **2025 was a breakout year for crypto IPOs** -- Circle ($1.1B raise, 168% first-day pop), Bullish (218% first-day surge), Gemini ($425M raise), and Figure ($787.5M raise) all went public, with Kraken and Consensys eyeing 2026 listings.
- **The SEC under Chairman Atkins has dramatically shifted the regulatory landscape**, issuing a comprehensive crypto asset taxonomy (March 17, 2026), proposing a token safe harbor with startup exemptions up to 4 years, and coordinating with the CFTC on jurisdictional clarity.
- **Tax treatment of token-to-equity conversion is almost certainly a taxable event** under current IRS guidance -- crypto-to-anything swaps are treated as dispositions, and Section 1031 like-kind exchange treatment has been unavailable for crypto since January 1, 2018.

## 1. The Across/Risk Labs "Bridge Across" Proposal

### 1.1 Overview and Rationale

On March 11, 2026, Risk Labs (the core development entity behind Across Protocol) posted "The Bridge Across" to the Across governance forum, proposing the protocol transition from a token-based DAO structure to a U.S. C-corporation.

Hart Lambur, CEO of Risk Labs, summarized the rationale bluntly: "Having a token generally hurts more than it helps." The team argued that the DAO structure "materially" impacted the protocol's ability to close enterprise deals, sign enforceable contracts, establish liability frameworks, and negotiate institutional revenue agreements.

This represents one of the first protocols to publicly argue that the token/DAO model is actively holding back business growth and that a traditional corporate structure would deliver more value to the same stakeholders.

**Sources:**
- [Across Protocol Forum: The Bridge Across](https://forum.across.to/t/the-bridge-across/2097)
- [CoinDesk: Across's ACX Rockets 80%](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/03/12/across-s-acx-rockets-80-massively-beating-bitcoin-on-plans-to-dump-its-dao-structure)
- [DL News: Crypto bridge Across soars](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/across-soars-as-foundation-proposes-turning-dao-into-company/)

### 1.2 Conversion Mechanics

**Entity Formation:**
- A newly formed entity, tentatively called "AcrossCo," would be structured as a U.S. C-corporation (Delaware incorporation implied by standard practice, though the specific state was not explicitly confirmed in available sources).
- AcrossCo would assume development, partnerships, commercialization, and intellectual property rights from the existing protocol.

**Token-to-Equity Conversion (1:1 Ratio):**
- All ACX token holders can exchange tokens for equity at a strict 1:1 token-to-share ratio. If you hold 1,000 ACX, you receive 1,000 shares (or equivalent SPV units).
- The proposal explicitly states: "All token holders -- institutional investors, employees, everyday token holders -- treated the same."

**Two Tiers of Equity Participation:**

| Holder Size | Mechanism | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Greater than 5 million ACX | Direct equity conversion | Convert tokens directly into shares of AcrossCo |
| 250,000 - 5 million ACX | No-fee SPV structure | Equity exposure through a Special Purpose Vehicle; minimum exchange size of ~250,000 ACX (~$10,000 at proposal-time prices) |

**Regulatory Caps on SPV Participation:**
- U.S. securities laws limit participation to approximately 100 accredited U.S. investors and approximately 500 international investors.
- U.S. investors must verify accredited investor status.
- This strongly suggests the conversion relies on Regulation D (Rule 506) exemptions for the equity offering.

**USDC Buyout Alternative:**
- Holders who do not want equity can sell ACX for USDC at $0.04375 per token -- a 25% premium to the 30-day volume-weighted average price at the time of the proposal.
- The buyout window would open within three months of the proposal passing and remain open for six months, funded by the protocol's liquid assets.

### 1.3 Equity Structure Details

**What is confirmed:**
- The conversion is at a 1:1 token-to-share ratio.
- Equal treatment of all holders regardless of size.
- SPV structure for smaller holders.

**What is NOT confirmed in available sources:**
- Whether the equity is common stock or preferred stock. Given the "equal treatment" language and the 1:1 conversion, common stock is the most likely structure, but this has not been explicitly stated in the sources I found.
- Specific voting rights, board representation, or governance rights of converted equity holders.
- Whether Paradigm or other existing investors would hold preferred stock with liquidation preferences, anti-dilution protections, or board seats.
- The specific law firm providing legal counsel on the transaction.
- The exact Delaware entity filing details.

**What happens to the Across DAO:**
- The DAO would effectively be dissolved. AcrossCo would assume all protocol operations, IP, and commercialization functions.
- The governance forum discussion and community call process suggest a structured wind-down rather than an abrupt shutdown.

## 2. Other Token-to-Equity and DAO-to-Corp Precedents

### 2.1 Uniswap: The "UNIfication" Hybrid Model

Uniswap has NOT pursued a full token-to-equity conversion, but its December 2025 "UNIfication" proposal represents the most significant restructuring of the DAO-Labs relationship in DeFi.

**Key details:**
- Proposal passed on December 25, 2025 with 99.9% support (125M+ UNI in favor, 742 against).
- Merged Uniswap Foundation operational functions into Uniswap Labs.
- Activated protocol fees directed to UNI token burns.
- Executed a one-time burn of 100 million UNI tokens.
- Established a 20 million UNI annual growth budget for Labs (via "DUNI" agreement), positioning Labs as a governance-accountable service provider.

**Structural significance:** UNIfication creates a hybrid DAO-corporate model where Labs is the operational entity but operates under DAO governance with expanded board accountability. This is distinct from Across's approach (full dissolution of the DAO) but shows the tension between decentralized governance and operational efficiency being resolved in favor of corporate-like structures.

**Uniswap Labs has always maintained a separate for-profit entity** that charges 0.15% on certain swaps through its proprietary frontend. This dual-entity model (Labs for-profit + DAO governance) has been a source of persistent tension.

**Sources:**
- [Yahoo Finance: Uniswap Governance Approves UNIfication](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/uniswap-governance-approves-unification-proposal-044254670.html)
- [CoinDesk: Uniswap's Token Burn Proposal](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/12/26/uniswap-s-token-burn-protocol-fee-proposal-backed-overwhelmingly-by-voters)
- [Uniswap Blog: UNIfication](https://blog.uniswap.org/unification)

### 2.2 MakerDAO/Sky: Token Migration, Not Equity Conversion

MakerDAO rebranded to Sky on August 27, 2024, introducing a new governance token (SKY) and stablecoin (USDS).

**Key details:**
- MKR converts to SKY at a 1:24,000 ratio; DAI converts to USDS at 1:1.
- As of early 2026, only ~14% of MKR had migrated to SKY.
- A "Delayed Upgrade Penalty" (1% decay in conversion ratio every 3 months starting September 18, 2025) was introduced to accelerate migration.
- Sky SubDAOs ("Sky Stars") function as semi-independent protocol units (e.g., Spark Protocol with $3B+ TVL).

**Structural significance:** Sky restructured within the DAO framework (new tokens, new SubDAOs) rather than converting to a corporate entity. This is a DAO-to-DAO restructuring, not a DAO-to-corp transition. The $60 million operating budget for 2026 and three-year vision for corporate treasury management products shows Sky is building enterprise features within a DAO structure rather than abandoning it.

**Sources:**
- [CoinDesk: MakerDAO Is Now Sky](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2024/08/27/makerdao-is-now-sky-as-7b-crypto-lender-rolls-out-new-stablecoin-governance-token/)
- [Blockworks: One Year Into Sky](https://blockworks.co/news/sky-dao-adoption)

### 2.3 dYdX: Decentralization via Chain Migration

dYdX took the opposite approach from Across -- moving further away from corporate control.

**Key details:**
- dYdX Trading Inc. (dYdX Labs) updated its charter to become a Public Benefit Corporation.
- dYdX Labs explicitly does not develop, control, or participate in the operation of any component of the dYdX protocol for public use.
- On dYdX Chain (an independent appchain), all fees go to validators and stakers -- not to a corporate entity.
- The dYdX Foundation handles governance and community functions.

**Structural significance:** dYdX represents the maximally decentralized model, where the corporate entity progressively reduces its role. This contrasts sharply with Across's approach.

**Sources:**
- [dYdX Foundation: Governance](https://www.dydx.foundation/)
- [CoinDesk: Uniswap DAO Outspoken Member Walks Away](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2025/05/07/why-one-of-uniswap-dao-most-outspoken-members-just-walked-away-in-frustration)

### 2.4 Summary: DAO-Corp Structural Spectrum

| Protocol | Approach | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Across Protocol | Full DAO dissolution, C-corp conversion | DAO --> Corp |
| Uniswap | UNIfication hybrid (Labs + DAO with fee switch) | DAO + Corp hybrid |

### 3.2 The Token-to-IPO Path: Does It Exist?

**Critical distinction:** None of the 2025 crypto IPOs followed a token-to-equity-to-IPO path. Every company that went public in 2025 was already structured as a traditional corporation:

- **Circle** had USDC (a stablecoin product) but the company itself was a Delaware corporation with traditional equity. USDC is not a governance token.
- **Coinbase** (listed 2021) never had a protocol token. Pure equity structure.
- **Bullish, Gemini, Figure** -- all traditional corporate structures.
- **Kraken** -- traditional corporate structure.

**The Across proposal, if executed, would be the first test case for a token-to-equity conversion that could theoretically precede an IPO.** However, no protocol has yet completed the full token --> equity --> IPO pipeline.

**Theoretical path:**
1. Protocol launches with governance token and DAO
2. Token-to-equity conversion (Across model): DAO dissolved, C-corp formed, token holders become shareholders
3. Traditional growth-stage equity rounds (Series A/B/C with VC firms)
4. IPO via traditional underwriting process

**Key obstacles to this path:**
- The equity resulting from token conversion would likely be illiquid private stock with transfer restrictions.
- SEC registration requirements for the initial conversion (Reg D caps at ~100 US accredited investors).
- Potential complications from having hundreds or thousands of existing shareholders (the "500 shareholder rule" under the Exchange Act could trigger public reporting requirements).
- Tax friction for token holders (see Section 4).

### 3.3 Which Projects Could Follow This Path?

Per The Block's analysis, the most likely candidates for token-to-equity conversion are:
- **B2B infrastructure and middleware protocols** where enterprise partnerships require enforceable contracts, SLAs, and clear counterparty liability.
- **Cross-chain bridges and interoperability protocols** (like Across) that serve institutional clients.
- Projects that **already operate like businesses** with a core team driving execution and revenue.

**Less likely candidates:**
- Protocols requiring **credible neutrality** (AMMs, lending markets) where corporate ownership creates conflicts.
- Maximally decentralized protocols with no identifiable core team.

## 4. Legal and Tax Implications

### 4.1 SEC Regulatory Framework (2026 Landscape)

The SEC under Chairman Paul Atkins has fundamentally shifted the regulatory environment for crypto assets in early 2026.

**March 17, 2026 -- Landmark Interpretive Release:**
- The SEC and CFTC jointly issued a comprehensive interpretation clarifying how federal securities laws apply to crypto assets.
- Established a coherent token taxonomy: digital commodities, digital collectibles, digital tools, stablecoins, and digital securities.
- Digital assets explicitly designed as securities (representing equity, debt, etc.) remain securities regardless of technological format.

**Token Safe Harbor Proposal:**
- Chairman Atkins proposed a "startup exemption" -- a time-limited registration exemption for investment contracts involving crypto assets lasting up to 4 years.
- During this period, developers could raise up to $5 million with notices to the Commission.
- A separate "fundraising exemption" for small offerings could allow raises up to $75 million in any 12-month period.
- An "investment contract safe harbor" would clarify when an issuer has completed essential managerial efforts, potentially allowing tokens to exit securities classification.

**Tokenized Securities Framework (January 28, 2026):**
- The SEC issued a joint statement confirming that tokenizing a security does not change its legal characterization.
- Issuers may permit security holders to convert securities between formats (onchain/offchain) without creating new securities.
- Stock is an "equity security" regardless of format.

**Sources:**
- [SEC: Regulation Crypto Assets: A Token Safe Harbor](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-remarks-regulation-crypto-assets-031726)
- [SEC: Statement on Tokenized Securities](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/corp-fin-statement-tokenized-securities-012826-statement-tokenized-securities)
- [Sidley Austin: SEC Releases Landmark Interpretation](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/03/sec-releases-landmark-interpretation-on-application-of-us-securities-laws-to-crypto-assets)

### 4.2 Is Token-to-Equity Conversion a Securities Offering?

**Almost certainly yes.** The issuance of equity (shares in AcrossCo) in exchange for tokens is an offer and sale of securities under the Securities Act. Key considerations:

**Regulation D (Rule 506) -- Most Likely Exemption:**
- The Across proposal's structure strongly implies reliance on Reg D: limited to ~100 US accredited investors, ~500 international investors, accredited investor verification required.
- Rule 506(b) allows sales to up to 35 non-accredited investors but prohibits general solicitation.
- Rule 506(c) allows unlimited accredited investors and permits general solicitation but requires "reasonable steps" to verify accreditation.
- Given the public nature of the governance proposal (posted on an open forum), Rule 506(c) seems most applicable.

**Regulation A+ (Mini-IPO):**
- Allows raises up to $75 million per year from non-accredited investors.
- Requires SEC qualification of an offering statement.
- More expensive and time-consuming than Reg D but allows broader participation.
- Not apparently the route Across is taking given the accredited investor limitations.

**Exchange Act Section 12(g) -- The 500 Shareholder Trigger:**
- Companies with more than $10 million in assets and more than 500 non-accredited shareholders (or 2,000 total shareholders) must register under the Exchange Act and file public reports.
- The SPV structure cleverly addresses this: by channeling smaller holders through a single SPV entity, the company may count the SPV as a single "holder of record" rather than counting each underlying beneficial owner.

### 4.3 Tax Implications for Token Holders

**Token-to-Equity Conversion is Almost Certainly a Taxable Event:**

Under current IRS guidance:
- Digital assets are treated as property for tax purposes.
- Any exchange, conversion, or disposition of a digital asset triggers a taxable event.
- When you exchange one crypto asset for another (or for equity), the IRS treats it as selling the first asset and buying the second.
- Capital gains or losses are calculated based on the fair market value at the time of the transaction minus the holder's cost basis.

**Section 1031 Like-Kind Exchange Treatment is NOT Available:**
- Since January 1, 2018, Section 1031 like-kind exchange treatment is limited exclusively to real property.
- The Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017 eliminated like-kind exchange treatment for personal property, including cryptocurrency.
- IRS Chief Counsel Advice (CCA 202124008) explicitly confirmed crypto-to-crypto exchanges do not qualify.
- Token-to-equity conversion would be even further from qualifying than crypto-to-crypto swaps.

**Practical Impact:**
- A token holder who bought ACX at $0.01 and converts at a fair market value of $0.06 would recognize $0.05/token in capital gains.
- The USDC buyout at $0.04375 is straightforwardly a sale/disposition.
- The equity conversion would be valued at the fair market value of the equity received (which may be difficult to determine for illiquid private company stock).

**Open question:** Could the conversion be structured as a tax-free reorganization under IRC Section 368? This would require the transaction to qualify as a statutory merger, consolidation, or exchange of stock -- categories designed for corporate reorganizations. The novel nature of token-to-equity conversion means there is no direct IRS guidance on whether this path is viable.

**Reporting Requirements (2026):**
- Brokers must report gross proceeds for transactions effected on or after January 1, 2025.
- Basis reporting on certain transactions becomes mandatory for transactions on or after January 1, 2026.

### 4.4 The GENIUS Act and Broader Legislation

**GENIUS Act (Signed July 18, 2025):**
- Establishes the first federal regulatory framework for USD-backed payment stablecoins.
- Requires 1:1 reserves (cash or short-term Treasurys) and monthly disclosure.
- Payment stablecoins are explicitly NOT classified as securities or commodities.
- Insured depositories overseen by their primary regulator; nonbank issuers overseen by the OCC.
- DeFi remains largely outside the reporting framework -- wrapping, LP transactions, staking, and lending are not covered by broker reporting rules.

**CLARITY Act (House Passed July 17, 2025; Stalled in Senate):**
- Would grant CFTC "exclusive jurisdiction" over digital commodity spot markets.
- Maintains SEC jurisdiction over "investment contract assets."
- Passed the House 294-134 with bipartisan support.
- Stalled in the Senate Banking Committee; disagreement over stablecoin yield has been the primary obstacle since January 2026.

**Impact on Token-to-Equity Conversions:**
- The GENIUS Act is largely irrelevant to token-to-equity conversions (it governs stablecoins, not governance tokens).
- The SEC's March 2026 interpretive release is more directly relevant, as it clarifies which tokens are securities, commodities, or utility tools.
- The proposed token safe harbor could theoretically affect how tokens are classified before conversion, but the safe harbor is designed for startup-phase tokens, not established protocols considering equity conversion.

## 5. The Venture Capital Angle

### 5.1 Paradigm's Position in Across

Paradigm led Across Protocol's $41 million funding round (completed in two phases in 2024), with participation from Bain Capital Crypto, Coinbase Ventures, and Multicoin Capital. Across has raised $51 million total through two token funding rounds.

**What Paradigm gains from conversion to equity:**

In a DAO/token structure, Paradigm holds governance tokens -- an asset class with:
- No liquidation preference
- No board seat rights
- No anti-dilution protection
- No information rights
- No drag-along/tag-along rights
- Limited enforceability of any contractual rights
- Token value subject to market speculation and liquidity dynamics

In a C-corp equity structure, Paradigm would typically receive:
- **Preferred stock** with 1x (or higher) liquidation preference
- **Board seat(s)** or board observer rights
- **Pro-rata rights** to participate in future rounds
- **Information rights** (quarterly financials, annual audits)
- **Anti-dilution protections** (weighted average or full ratchet)
- **Protective provisions** (veto over key corporate actions: new fundraising, M&A, changes to charter)
- **Drag-along/tag-along rights** for exit scenarios
- **Registration rights** (demand or piggyback rights for eventual IPO)

**However -- the Across proposal states "all token holders treated the same."** If this is strictly true, it means Paradigm would receive common stock at the same 1:1 ratio as every other holder, without special preferred stock rights. This would be unusual for a VC firm and raises questions:

1. Did Paradigm negotiate separate side agreements?
2. Will AcrossCo do a subsequent preferred equity round after conversion?
3. Is the "equal treatment" language aspirational or legally binding?

I cannot verify the answers to these questions from available sources. This is a critical area for further investigation.

### 5.2 The SAFE/SAFT-to-Token-to-Equity Pipeline

The evolution of crypto fundraising instruments tells the story of the industry's relationship with securities law:

**Phase 1 (2013-2017): Direct Token Sales / ICOs**
- Tokens sold directly to the public, often without any securities compliance.
- The SEC's 2017 DAO Report established that many tokens are securities.

**Phase 2 (2017-2020): SAFTs (Simple Agreement for Future Tokens)**
- Developed as a crypto adaptation of Y Combinator's SAFE instrument.
- Investors receive rights to future tokens, not the tokens themselves.
- Designed to comply with Reg D by selling to accredited investors.
- Tokens delivered after network launch, theoretically after "sufficient decentralization."

**Phase 3 (2020-2024): SAFE + Token Warrant / Token Side Letter**
- The dominant structure in crypto VC by 2024-2025.
- Investors receive BOTH equity (via SAFE) AND token rights (via a token warrant or side letter).
- This gives VCs optionality: if the company does an IPO, they have equity; if it launches a token, they have token allocation.

**Phase 4 (2025-2026): Hybrid Equity + Token Structures and Conversion**
- Most U.S. pre-seed and seed crypto deals use the SAFE (equity) as the primary instrument.
- The Across proposal represents a potential Phase 5: retroactive conversion from tokens back to equity, essentially unwinding Phase 2-3 structures.

**Source:**
- [Outlier Ventures: SAFE, SAFT, SAFE+T](https://outlierventures.io/article/safe-saft-safet/)
- [Pulley: What Is a SAFT?](https://pulley.com/guides/saft-simple-agreement-for-future-tokens)

### 5.3 Is This Fundamentally About VCs Wanting Traditional Equity?

The cynical view: Token-to-equity conversion allows VCs to exchange an illiquid, speculative governance token (with no legal protections) for preferred equity with full corporate protections, board control, and a clear path to IPO exit.

The charitable view: Enterprise clients and institutional partners genuinely require corporate counterparties for enforceable contracts, and the token/DAO model creates real friction that limits protocol growth.

**The truth is likely both.** The practical limitations of DAO structures for B2B partnerships are real and well-documented. But the specific conversion mechanics -- particularly whether VCs receive preferred stock vs. common, and whether side agreements exist -- will reveal the true balance of interests.

**Key data point:** The crypto VC market invested $4.8 billion in Q1 2025 alone, the highest since Q3 2022. Most VCs now pursue hybrid equity + token structures precisely because they want optionality. The Across conversion represents a VC resolving that optionality in favor of equity.

### Strategic Implications

For anyone building in DeFi with an eye toward the 2028 cycle:

1. **The DAO-to-corp pipeline is now real.** Across is the test case. If it succeeds, expect a wave of similar conversions, particularly among B2B infrastructure protocols.

2. **The IPO window is open.** Circle's 168% first-day pop and the broader 2025 crypto IPO wave prove institutional appetite for crypto equity exists.

3. **Build with dual-track optionality.** The winning structure for a new protocol may be: SAFE + token warrant at founding, token launch for network bootstrap, with the corporate entity always maintained for an eventual equity conversion or IPO if the token model hits limits.

4. **The SEC is actively building frameworks** that accommodate both tokens and equity, including conversion between them. The regulatory headwinds of 2022-2024 have shifted to regulatory tailwinds in 2025-2026.

5. **Tax friction is the biggest unresolved obstacle.** A taxable event on conversion creates friction that could deter participation. Legislative or IRS guidance clarifying tax treatment of token-to-equity conversions would significantly accelerate adoption.
